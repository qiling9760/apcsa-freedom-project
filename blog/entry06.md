# Entry 6
##### 6/7/26

### Context
I created a match-3 puzzle game using a JavaScript tool called [Phaser](https://phaser.io/). To play the game, the player needs to use the mouse to swap adjacent sprites and create matches of three or more identical sprites horizontally or vertically. My remix to the game is that the sprites will stay in their new positions after a swap, even if they do not make a match. I presented my game to my class and to other students in the expositions. 

### Takeaways from in-class presentation
I made a [slideshow](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1VTlK6O89qkjLvx-MZSBPfKbWOPS2eTP3Vno-coBnRrU/edit?usp=drivesdk) to explain my process of creating the game for the presentation. Before I started creating my slideshow, I [planned](../prep/presentation.md) out what I should include in it.

During the presentation, it is important to slow down in the explaination. Speaking too quickly can make me forget what I was going to say next. I should stop and think when I come across a confusing part of the code, so that I can organize my thoughts and explain it clearly. At the end of my presentation, I should say "Thank you" to let the audience know that I have finished presenting.

### Takeaways from giving Expo elevator pitch
I needed to prepare an elevator pitch to explain my game to non-APCSA students and the judges at the exposition.
